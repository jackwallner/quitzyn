## How to Quit Zyn: Withdrawal Timeline and What to Expect

By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 23, 2026

Zyn withdrawal is the discomfort that follows stopping nicotine pouches, because regular nicotine use leads to dependence. Most people feel the worst of Zyn withdrawal in the first few days, with symptoms peaking around day 3 and generally easing over the first month. Expect cravings, irritability, trouble sleeping and trouble concentrating. The timelines come from tobacco research, because pouches are newer. ## Why does quitting Zyn feel rough?

Nicotine pouches deliver nicotine, and regular nicotine use leads to dependence, which is why stopping brings withdrawal. The FDA authorized 20 Zyn products for sale to adults in January 2025, but authorization does not mean safe. The agency's own line is that there is no safe tobacco product. If you want out, the discomfort is predictable and it passes.

## What is the Zyn withdrawal timeline?

Withdrawal usually peaks in the first 3 days, is worst in the first week and eases over the first month. Most published timelines come from research on cigarettes and other tobacco, because nicotine pouches are newer and less studied. Nicotine withdrawal works the same way in the body, so the National Cancer Institute (NCI) timeline is the best guide available. Your own course will vary with how much and how long you used.

| When | What the NCI describes |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 hours after the last use | Cravings can start, and may be frequent for the next few days or weeks. |
| Days 1 to 3 | Symptoms are usually worst in the first week and peak in the first 3 days. Anxiety usually builds over these three days. Mild low mood can begin on day one. |
| Week 1 | Anger, frustration and irritability peak within the first week, then may last 2 to 4 weeks. |
| Weeks 2 to 4 | Intensity usually drops over the first month. Anxiety can last several weeks. Mild low mood typically lasts a couple of weeks and is gone within a month. |
| After a month | Some people still have symptoms for several months. |

Smokefree.gov, run by the NCI, puts it more simply: for most people the worst symptoms last a few days to a couple of weeks, and that stretch is when you are most likely to slip.

## What are the symptoms of Zyn withdrawal?

Common symptoms are nicotine cravings, anger and irritability, trouble concentrating, insomnia, restlessness, anxiety, low mood, and hunger or increased appetite, according to the NCI. Less common are headaches, fatigue, dizziness, coughing, mouth ulcers and constipation. The appetite change is why many people who quit notice weight gain, so plan for snacks and movement instead of being surprised by it.

## What steps help you quit Zyn?

Set a quit date, clear out your supply, plan for your triggers, keep your mouth and hands busy, and use free support. The steps below follow NCI and Smokefree.gov guidance.

1. **Pick a quit date and clear out your supply.** Remove pouches from your car, desk, jacket and bag, so a bad moment does not turn into a trip to the store.
2. **Plan for your triggers.** The NCI advises avoiding situations you associate with use, at least for a while. Think about your usual pouch moments (after meals, driving, work breaks) and decide what you will do instead.
3. **Keep your mouth and hands busy.** The NCI suggests carrots, sugar-free gum or hard candy.
4. **Move.** A walk is enough. Both the NCI and Smokefree.gov recommend physical activity for restlessness and cravings.
5. **Protect sleep.** Smokefree.gov suggests a consistent bedtime and wake time, no screens in the bedroom, and less caffeine, alcohol and late heavy meals.
6. **Remind yourself it is temporary.** Cravings, anger and anxiety all fade. Breathing or relaxation exercises can carry you through a spike.
7. **Use free support.** Call 1-800-QUIT-NOW (1-800-784-8669) to reach a trained tobacco counselor. Text CRAVE to 47848 for support during a craving, or QUIT to 47848 for a 6 to 8 week text program.

## Should you quit cold turkey, taper or use medication?

The sources cited here do not rank cold turkey against cutting down gradually. What the CDC does say is that counseling and medication together give you the best chance of quitting for good. Over-the-counter nicotine replacement (patch, gum, lozenge) and prescription options exist, and the NCI notes that combining a long-acting form with a short-acting one can help. The NCI also says nicotine replacement is not recommended during pregnancy. A doctor or pharmacist can tell you what fits you, including how to use it.

## When should you get more help?

If low mood or anxiety gets worse or does not go away, talk to a health care professional. If you or someone you know is in distress or having thoughts of suicide, call or text 988 any time.
